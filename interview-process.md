# Microsoft Interview Process

The Microsoft engineering interview is commonly reported to run in four main stages. A recruiter call comes first, then an online assessment or a technical phone screen, then the loop, then team discussion and the offer. Reported timelines range from two weeks to two months, and four to eight weeks is common.

Not every candidate sees every stage. The steps after the first screen are reported to vary by role and by team, so ask your recruiter before each stage.

## The stages in order

| Stage | Reported format | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Application or referral | Resume review | Whether a recruiter contacts you |
| Recruiter screen | 15 to 30 minutes by phone | Role fit, the level to interview at, and the group to route you to |
| Online assessment | 2 or 3 problems in 60 to 90 minutes | Whether an early-career candidate reaches a live interview |
| Technical phone screen | 45 to 60 minutes of live coding | Whether an experienced candidate reaches the loop |
| The loop | 4 or 5 interviews of about 60 minutes each | A hire or no-hire recommendation from each interviewer |
| Final senior interview (AA) | 45 to 60 minutes | Whether the hire meets the standard used across teams |
| Team discussion and offer | Calls with the recruiter | The team, the level, and the offer terms |

## Recruiter screen

The recruiter asks about your background, the work you want next, and when you are available. Microsoft gives each engineer a numbered level, and the recruiter uses your years of work and current scope to place you. See [levels](levels.md).

Microsoft hires engineers into many separate groups, including cloud, operating systems, productivity software, gaming, and security. A specific answer about the products that interest you helps the recruiter choose a team.

## Online assessment or phone screen

The online assessment (OA) is a timed coding test that you take alone in a browser, graded by hidden test cases. It is mostly sent to students, interns, and new graduates. Experienced candidates often skip it and start with a live technical screen on a shared editor or on Microsoft Teams. The [coding interview](coding-interview.md) page covers both formats.

## The loop

The loop is the set of four or five back-to-back interviews with the hiring team. A commonly reported split is two or three [coding interviews](coding-interview.md) and one [design interview](system-design-interview.md). [Behavioral questions](behavioral-interview.md) take about ten minutes of each interview, and some loops add a separate behavioral interview.

After the loop, each interviewer files written feedback with a hire or no-hire recommendation. The interviewers then meet to discuss the candidate, and no single interviewer is reported to have the final decision.

## The final senior interview (AA)

AA stands for "as appropriate". The name comes from an older practice in which the last interview happened only when the earlier interviewers thought it was appropriate.

| Question | Commonly reported answer |
|---|---|
| Who runs it? | A senior leader from outside the hiring team |
| When? | As the last interview of the loop, or 1 to 2 weeks after it |
| What is asked? | One project in depth, judgment, disagreement, scope, and sometimes one open technical question |
| Can it change the result? | Yes. This leader is reported to be able to reject a candidate the loop supported |
| Does everyone have one? | No. Some teams skip it, and some roles decide inside the loop |

Reports differ on whether the round is a real evaluation or a talk about the team, so prepare for the evaluation.

## After the loop

- A decision is commonly reported within one to three weeks.
- A verbal offer is reported three to five business days after an AA.
- Silence past three weeks is common. Reported causes include a paused role, team matching, and an interviewer on leave.
- Send one short status message seven to ten business days after the loop.
- Team discussions still count, because the hiring manager is deciding the team and the level.
