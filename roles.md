# Microsoft Interviews for Other Roles

The loop described in this guide is the software engineer loop. The sources for this guide cover three other cases: early-career hiring, specialist engineering teams, and the cloud solution architect role. The format after the first screen is reported to vary by role, so confirm each stage with your recruiter.

## How the cases compare

| Case | Screen | Main difference from the standard loop |
|---|---|---|
| Student, intern, or new graduate | Online assessment | A timed test first, and class design in place of system design |
| Specialist engineering team | Live coding screen | One interview may be a technical deep dive in the team's area |
| Cloud solution architect | Recruiter screen | Architecture and customer questions, with a possible presentation |

## Students, interns, and new graduates

Most online assessments (OAs) are reported to go to this group. The OA is a timed coding test of two or three problems, graded by hidden test cases, and intern roles sometimes receive one problem.

- The invitation usually allows three to seven days to start the test.
- The reported bar is two solutions that pass every test.
- University hiring runs in batches, and teams are often matched after the interviews.
- The design interview is often a class design question, such as a parking lot.
- Candidates who do not pass can usually apply again in the next hiring cycle.

See the [coding interview](coding-interview.md) page for how the OA is graded.

## Specialist engineering teams

Microsoft hires engineers into separate groups, including cloud, operating systems, productivity software, gaming, and security. Depending on the role, one interview is reported to be a technical deep dive. A technical deep dive is an interview on one field, such as databases, web technologies, machine learning, or security.

The sources do not describe a separate loop for each of these teams. Ask your recruiter whether the team adds a deep dive, and in which field.

## Cloud solution architect

Microsoft calls its solutions architect role cloud solution architect, and the job is reported to start mainly after the customer has signed. The loop is therefore likely to ask whether you can turn a signed agreement into running workloads.

Microsoft publishes no loop for this role. One commonly reported version has four rounds.

| Round | Reported length | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | About 30 minutes | Background, motivation, and why Microsoft |
| Technical and architecture | 45 to 60 minutes | A design with trade-offs, often on a case or a whiteboard |
| Behavioral and customer-facing | 45 to 60 minutes | Past work, with a focus on the customer |
| Hiring manager | 45 to 60 minutes | Fit and business judgment, sometimes with a presentation |

Three differences from the software engineer loop matter most.

- **Less algorithm coding.** The technical round is an architecture discussion, not a coding problem.
- **A customer in every answer.** Postings for the role use the phrase "trusted advisor". Prepare a story in which you told a customer no and offered a better option.
- **Adoption as the measure.** Postings list "drive consumption growth" as a duty. Consumption is the amount of the platform that a customer uses and pays for each month.

Growth mindset applies here as well, and the [behavioral interview](behavioral-interview.md) page explains how to end each story with what you learned.

## Roles this page does not cover

The sources do not describe a Microsoft loop for engineering managers, product managers, or data scientists, so this page leaves those roles out.
