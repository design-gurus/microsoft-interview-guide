Three things make the Microsoft interview different from other large technology companies. Behavioral questions appear in nearly every interview instead of one dedicated hour. The design interview can be system design or class design, depending on the team and the level. Many loops also end with a senior interviewer from outside the hiring team, a round that candidates call the "as appropriate" (AA) interview.

This repository is a short, free guide to the whole process. It covers each stage, the numbered levels from 59 upward, a question bank, and a four-week plan. Each page takes about three minutes to read. The process details are commonly reported by candidates and can differ by team, so confirm the format with your recruiter.

This guide is independent. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by Microsoft.

## The loop at a glance

The loop is the final set of back-to-back interviews. At Microsoft it is commonly reported as four or five interviews of about an hour each.

| Stage | Reported length | What it measures | Guide page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | 15 to 30 minutes | Background, role fit, level, and timing | [Interview process](interview-process.md) |
| Online assessment (early-career roles) | 60 to 90 minutes | Correct code, edge cases, and speed | [Coding interview](coding-interview.md) |
| Technical phone screen (experienced roles) | 45 to 60 minutes | Problem solving, code quality, and communication | [Coding interview](coding-interview.md) |
| Loop: coding | 2 or 3 interviews of about 60 minutes | One well-scoped problem solved and tested | [Coding interview](coding-interview.md) |
| Loop: design | 1 interview of about 60 minutes | System design or class design | [System design interview](system-design-interview.md) |
| Behavioral questions | Part of nearly every interview | Growth mindset, collaboration, and customer focus | [Behavioral interview](behavioral-interview.md) |
| Final senior interview (AA) | 45 to 60 minutes | Judgment and a consistent standard across teams | [Interview process](interview-process.md) |
| Team discussion and offer | Calls with the recruiter | Level, team match, and offer terms | [Levels](levels.md) |

## FAQ

### How many rounds are in a Microsoft interview?

Most candidates report a recruiter screen, one coding screen, and a loop of four or five interviews. That is six to eight conversations in total. Senior candidates often have one more interview with a leader from another group. See the [interview process](interview-process.md).

### How hard is the Microsoft interview?

Reported difficulty is moderate for a large technology company. Coding questions are usually at medium difficulty, and the design interview gets harder at higher levels. A weak design interview at a senior level can lead to an offer at a lower level. See [levels](levels.md).

### How long does the Microsoft hiring process take?

Reports range from about two weeks to two months, and four to eight weeks is common. Most of the waiting happens before the loop is scheduled and after it ends. A decision after the loop is commonly reported within one to three weeks.

### What coding questions does Microsoft ask?

Microsoft asks data structure and algorithm questions. The topics that repeat are arrays, strings, linked lists, trees, graphs, hash maps, recursion, and dynamic programming. The [question bank](question-bank.md) lists 25 problems, and 21 of them link to a lesson.

### Does Microsoft ask system design?

Yes, mainly from level 63 upward. Early-career loops often replace it with a class design question, such as a parking lot. Ask your recruiter which kind of design round to expect. See the [system design interview](system-design-interview.md).

### What is the AA interview at Microsoft?

AA stands for "as appropriate". It is an extra interview with a senior leader from outside the hiring team, held after the main loop. Not every candidate has one, and that leader is reported to be able to reject a candidate the loop supported.
