# Microsoft Levels and the Interview

Microsoft gives every engineer a number, and software engineering runs from 59 to 80. Each title covers two or three numbers, so the number matters more than the title. Coding carries most of the decision at level 61 and below, and the design interview often decides the level at 63 and above.

These levels are commonly reported by employees and candidates. They are not an official Microsoft publication.

## The ladder

| Level | Title | Typical scope |
|---|---|---|
| **59 to 60** | Software Engineer (SDE) | Well-defined tasks inside one team, with regular review |
| **61 to 62** | Software Engineer II (SDE II) | Features built end to end with little supervision |
| **63 to 64** | Senior Software Engineer | A project or problem area owned for one team |
| **65 to 67** | Principal Software Engineer | Work that spans several teams or a whole organization |
| **68 to 69** | Partner | Direction across a whole product line or division |
| **70** | Distinguished Engineer | Company-wide technical direction |
| **80** | Technical Fellow | Company-wide and industry-wide influence |

The numbers start at 59 because Microsoft uses one scale for every job family, not only engineering. For that reason, a Microsoft number cannot be compared directly with a number at another company. Level 63 is roughly the senior level elsewhere, and the Principal title at 65 to 67 roughly matches staff level.

Level 63 is the terminal level, which means an engineer can hold it for a whole career without pressure to be promoted.

## How the interview changes by level

| Level | Screen | Design interview | What carries the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 59 to 60 | Online assessment, common for students and new graduates | Often a class design question | Coding |
| 61 to 62 | Online assessment or live coding screen | Class design or system design, depending on the team | Coding, then design |
| 63 to 64 | Live coding screen | System design, with follow-up questions on scale and failure | System design and scope of past work |
| 65 to 67 | Live coding screen | System design, at the widest scope | Design, plus evidence of work across teams |

The sources for this guide describe the loop in detail up to level 64. For 65 and above, they describe the scope of the job more than the interview. The last row is therefore an inference from that scope.

The final senior interview, called AA, is reported more often for senior candidates, and not every candidate has one.

## Down-leveling

Down-leveling is an offer at a level below the one you interviewed for. Senior candidates report that a weak design interview can lead to a lower-level offer instead of a rejection.

Two things are reported to carry the most weight between adjacent levels.

- **Scope of past work.** A level 63 engineer owns a system, and a level 61 engineer owns a feature. Describe what you owned, not what you contributed to.
- **System design performance.** At 63 and above, this interview often decides the level.

## What to ask the recruiter

1. Which number is this loop for? A Senior Software Engineer posting can be a 63 loop or a 64 loop.
2. Is the design interview system design or class design?
3. Is there a final senior interview, and who runs it?

Ask for the number before the loop is scheduled. Interviewers are reported to know which level they are assessing against, and levels rarely change after the interviews.
