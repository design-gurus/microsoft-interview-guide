# Microsoft Behavioral Interview

Microsoft is reported to grade behavior in nearly every interview, not in one dedicated hour. At many levels each interviewer spends about the first ten minutes on your past work. The main trait is growth mindset, which means treating ability as something you build by learning.

## How Microsoft frames the round

Microsoft's careers site is reported to name growth mindset, collaboration across teams, and customer focus as the traits it looks for. Candidates describe the culture with one phrase: be a "learn-it-all", not a "know-it-all". The final senior interview, called AA, is reported to rely heavily on behavioral questions.

Interviewers are also reported to score a set of competencies. A competency is a named skill that an interviewer rates from the evidence in your answers.

| Competency | What a strong story shows |
|---|---|
| Growth mindset | A real failure, the feedback you received, and the behavior you changed |
| Collaboration | Work that crossed team boundaries |
| Influencing for impact | A result you achieved through people who did not report to you |
| Customer focus | A decision traced back to its effect on a user or customer |
| Drive for results | A result delivered despite a serious obstacle |
| Adaptability | A plan you rebuilt when priorities changed |
| Judgment | A decision made with incomplete information |
| Inclusion | A specific action that helped other people contribute |

Inclusion is reported as an explicit interview dimension at Microsoft. Prepare one specific story for it, such as changing a meeting format so that quieter members contributed.

## Question themes

- **Feedback and learning:** "Describe the most useful critical feedback you have received. What did you do with it?"
- **Being wrong:** "Tell me about a technical decision you were wrong about. What did you do next?"
- **Conflict:** "Describe a conflict with a coworker. How did you resolve it?"
- **Influence:** "Tell me about a time you had to influence a team you had no authority over."
- **Customer:** "Tell me about a time you advocated for the customer against an easier internal option."
- **Motivation:** "Why Microsoft, and why this team?"

The [question bank](question-bank.md) lists 15 behavioral questions by theme.

## STAR, plus what you learned

STAR is a four-part structure for a behavioral answer: Situation, Task, Action, Result. It is taught in the lesson [The STAR method](https://www.designgurus.io/course-play/grokking-behavioral-interview/doc/the-star-method?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=microsoft-interview-guide&utm_content=behavioral-interview).

At Microsoft, add a fifth part: what you learned and what you changed afterward. The grading is reported to reward that step, so an answer that stops at the result loses credit.

| Part | Length | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | 2 sentences | The team, the system, and the problem |
| Task | 1 sentence | What you were responsible for |
| Action | 4 to 6 sentences | What you did, using "I" and not "we" |
| Result | 1 or 2 sentences | The outcome, with one number |
| Learning | 1 or 2 sentences | The behavior you changed afterward |

## How to prepare stories

1. Write six stories that cover feedback, conflict, ownership, customer impact, and learning.
2. Include two real failures. A disguised success is reported to score poorly.
3. Include one story where another person's idea changed your approach.
4. Put one number in each result, then state who benefited.
5. Prepare three deep project stories for the AA: one build, one failure, and one disagreement.
6. Keep each answer near two minutes, then stop and let the interviewer ask.

For the AA, state the result first and the reasoning second. A senior leader usually wants the outcome before the detail.
