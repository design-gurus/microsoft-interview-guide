# Common Mistakes in the Microsoft Interview

The most common mistakes in the Microsoft loop come from preparing for a different company. Candidates prepare one behavioral round, one kind of design question, and no final senior interview. The ten mistakes below follow from the reported format.

## The ten mistakes in one table

| # | Mistake | Stage where it costs most |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Preparing behavioral answers for one round only | Every loop interview |
| 2 | Ending a story at the result | Behavioral questions and the AA |
| 3 | Offering a disguised success as a failure | Behavioral questions |
| 4 | Not asking which kind of design round it is | Design interview |
| 5 | Leaving out security, tenants, and cost | System design at 63 and above |
| 6 | Submitting online assessment code without your own tests | Online assessment |
| 7 | Coding in silence | Live coding interviews |
| 8 | Treating the AA as a formality | Final senior interview |
| 9 | Not asking for the level number | Recruiter screen |
| 10 | Reading silence as rejection | After the loop |

## Behavioral mistakes

**1. Preparing behavioral answers for one round only.** Behavior is reported to be graded in nearly every interview, and many interviewers spend the first ten minutes on past work. Bring six stories, so that you do not repeat one story to three interviewers.

**2. Ending a story at the result.** STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. Microsoft's grading is reported to reward one more step, which is what you learned and what you changed afterward. See the [behavioral interview](behavioral-interview.md) page.

**3. Offering a disguised success as a failure.** An answer such as "I cared too much" is reported to score poorly. Growth mindset means treating ability as something you build by learning, so show a real failure and a specific change in behavior.

## Design mistakes

**4. Not asking which kind of design round it is.** The design interview can be system design or class design. A candidate who prepared only a service architecture may be asked to write classes for an elevator. Ask the recruiter beforehand, and confirm in the first minute of the interview.

**5. Leaving out security, tenants, and cost.** Many Microsoft customers are large organizations, and each one is a tenant that shares the service with others. Interviewers are reported to expect encryption, identity, and access control in the design, and they may ask how it controls cost. Raise all three topics before the interviewer does.

## Coding mistakes

**6. Submitting online assessment code without your own tests.** Hidden test cases grade the online assessment, and a correct solution that times out is reported to score like a wrong one. Test empty input, one element, duplicates, and the largest allowed value before you submit.

**7. Coding in silence.** In a live interview, the explanation is graded together with the code. State the plan before typing, state the time and space cost, and trace one small example at the end.

## Process mistakes

**8. Treating the AA as a formality.** AA means "as appropriate", and it is a final interview with a senior leader from outside the hiring team. That person is reported to read the earlier feedback first and can reject a candidate the loop supported. Prepare three deep project stories, and expect questions on the parts of the loop that looked weak.

**9. Not asking for the level number.** A posting that says Senior Software Engineer can be a 63 loop or a 64 loop, and levels are reported to change rarely after the interviews. Ask for the number before the loop is scheduled. See [levels](levels.md).

**10. Reading silence as rejection.** A wait of more than three weeks after the loop is commonly reported, and the causes include a paused role, team matching, and the scheduling of an AA. Send one status message after seven to ten business days, and keep your other interviews running.
